## Summary
MDN's overview of threat modeling as a structured security practice for web applications. The guide introduces key terminology, the four questions from the Threat Modeling Manifesto, frameworks like STRIDE and LINDDUN, and the ERTA response approach. Threat modeling is framed as an ongoing cross-functional activity, not a one-time security audit.

## Key takeaways
- Threat modeling should happen early in development and be revisited continuously — it is not a one-time exercise.
- The four key questions: What are we working on? What can go wrong? What are we going to do about it? Did we do a good enough job?
- System modeling covers components, assets, data flows and trust boundaries, external dependencies, and stakeholders.
- Use OWASP Top 10, STRIDE, or LINDDUN frameworks to systematically enumerate threats.
- Respond to threats with ERTA: Eliminate, Reduce, Transfer, or Accept.
- Cross-functional teams (not just security specialists) produce stronger threat models.
