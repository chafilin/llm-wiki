## Summary
MIT Media Lab preprint (Kosmyna et al., 2025, under review). 54 university-age participants wrote essays across 3 sessions under one of three conditions: ChatGPT only, Google Search only, or no tools (Brain-only). EEG measured neural connectivity throughout; post-session interviews probed memory, ownership, and satisfaction. The central finding: brain engagement scales inversely with external AI support, and this deficit compounds across sessions — hence "cognitive debt."

## Key takeaways
- **Memory collapse in LLM group (Session 1):** 83.3% of LLM users could not quote anything from their own essay minutes after writing it; 0% produced a correct verbatim quote. Search Engine and Brain-only groups: ~11% failure rate, ~88% correct quotes. Effect size Cramer's V = 0.82, p < 0.001.
- **Ownership fragmentation:** LLM group was the only group with reports of zero ownership. Brain-only: 89% claimed full ownership. LLM: 50% full, 33% partial, 17% none.
- **EEG — brain connectivity:** Neural coupling (measured via Dynamic Directed Transfer Function across alpha/beta/theta/delta bands) was weakest in LLM group, intermediate in Search Engine, strongest in Brain-only — a clean dose-response relationship.
- **Session 4 crossover (clever design):** Participants switched groups for an optional 4th session without warning, rewriting a prior-session topic. LLM→Brain showed weaker neural activity and under-engagement. Brain→LLM showed higher memory recall and re-engagement of visual processing networks. Prior habit shaped the brain's response even in the switched condition.
- **NLP analysis:** LLM essays were stylistically homogeneous and less personal. AI judge scored all groups similarly; human teachers preferred Brain-only essays.
- **Satisfaction paradox:** LLM group reported 94% satisfaction with their essays despite producing the weakest recall and lowest ownership scores. They felt good about work they couldn't remember.
- **Literature finding:** LLMs reduce germane cognitive load most — the kind needed for schema construction and long-term learning — not extraneous load. This is the bad kind of reduction.
- **Competence gap:** Higher-competence learners use LLMs strategically while staying engaged; lower-competence learners just offload, compounding their disadvantage.
- **Metacognitive laziness:** The authors use this term for students offloading cognitive and metacognitive responsibility to AI, bypassing the intellectual effort required for internalization.
